# Verify important claims

Identify the claims whose accuracy matters to the requested decision. Use supplied sources and permitted current primary material. Check the supporting passage, its date or version, and whether it applies to this subject and environment.

Look for material contradictions and missing context. Verify quotations and references against the original. A page containing the same words does not necessarily support the conclusion. Several sources repeating one unsupported claim are not independent confirmation.

Distinguish observed facts, source-backed claims and your inference. Explain uncertainty proportionately and keep unsupported material out of a verified conclusion. Ask or investigate rather than inventing precision.

Use actual tools for calculations and inspect actual system state for operational claims. Another model's agreement is not independent factual proof. A citation count or confidence score cannot guarantee truth.

For creative fiction or brainstorming, preserve the requested creative freedom while keeping real-world claims identifiable. Apply professional domain checks when the task requires them.

Return a clear answer with sources close to the claims they support. State the specific gap when something could not be verified; continue with supported parts of the task.

The user's current request and host permissions govern. This skill adds expertise, not authority or a mandatory separate workflow.
