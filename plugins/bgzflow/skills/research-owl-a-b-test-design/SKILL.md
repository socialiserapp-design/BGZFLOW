# A/B Test Design

Use this method only for the current research question. Reuse the existing brief, applicable user answers and private records; several selected methods contribute to one investigation, not repeated interviews. Inspect permitted actual evidence and preserve source links. Do not invent participants, quotations, emotions, findings, measurements or approvals; mark hypotheses and missing data explicitly. Planning a study does not authorise recruitment, messages, recording, tracking installation, spending, account changes or product changes. The current task and host permissions govern; shared automatic records are not proof that a study ran.
## What You Do
You design A/B tests with clear hypotheses, controlled variants, appropriate metrics, and statistical rigor.
## Test Structure
### 1. Hypothesis
Structured as: 'If we [change], then [outcome] will [improve/decrease] because [rationale].'
### 2. Variants
- Control (A): current design
- Treatment (B): proposed change
- Define the treatment and causal question. One change can simplify interpretation; a justified factorial or bundled treatment design can also fit
### 3. Primary Metric
The single most important measure of success. Must be measurable, relevant, and sensitive to the change.
### 4. Secondary Metrics
Supporting measures and guardrail metrics to detect unintended consequences.
### 5. Sample Size
Based on: minimum detectable effect, baseline conversion rate, statistical significance level (typically 95%), and power (typically 80%).
### 6. Duration
Predefine allocation, sampling, observation windows and stopping rules from traffic, business cycles, expected effect and risk. One or two weeks is not a universal minimum
## Common Pitfalls
- Peeking at results before completion
- Too many variants at once
- Metric not sensitive enough to detect change
- Sample size too small
- Not accounting for novelty effects
- Ignoring segmentation effects
## When Not to A/B Test
- Very low traffic (insufficient sample)
- Ethical concerns with withholding improvement
- Foundational changes that affect everything
- When qualitative insight is more valuable
## Best Practices
- One hypothesis per test
- Predeclare material hypothesis, assignment unit, metrics, analysis and stopping rules in the existing experiment record; no new paperwork package
- Do not opportunistically stop on a favourable result; use the predeclared fixed-horizon or valid sequential design and actual safety boundaries
- Analyze segments after overall results
- Retain relevant learning, including negative outcomes, in the existing private record; sharing requires actual authority
