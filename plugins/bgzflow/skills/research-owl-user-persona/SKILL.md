# User Persona

Use this method only for the current research question. Reuse the existing brief, applicable user answers and private records; several selected methods contribute to one investigation, not repeated interviews. Inspect permitted actual evidence and preserve source links. Do not invent participants, quotations, emotions, findings, measurements or approvals; mark hypotheses and missing data explicitly. Planning a study does not authorise recruitment, messages, recording, tracking installation, spending, account changes or product changes. The current task and host permissions govern; shared automatic records are not proof that a study ran.

Create comprehensive user personas grounded in research data for product and UX design.

## Context


Inspect the available research first. An authorised product URL can establish product facts, not customer behaviour.

## Domain Context

- Personas (Alan Cooper, About Face): Archetypical users based on behavioral patterns, not demographics alone.
- Each persona is an evidence-based analytical composite, not a real participant or invented testimonial.
- Personas should be grounded in actual research data, not assumptions.
- Include behavioral variables, goals (life goals, experience goals, end goals), and frustrations.

## Instructions

The user will describe their product and available research data. Work through these steps:

1. **Gather inputs**: Confirm the product, target audience, and available research data. Ask for clarification if anything is ambiguous.
2. **Identify behavioral patterns**: Analyze the research data to find clusters of behaviors, motivations, and needs.
3. **Define the personas supported by the research and needed for this decision** — for each persona, include:
   - Neutral pseudonymous label and supported behavioural summary. A composite name or illustration is optional and clearly fictional; do not invent quotations
   - Relevant observed context such as role and tool familiarity; do not invent demographic precision
   - Goals: what they want to achieve (functional, emotional, social)
   - Frustrations: current pain points and unmet needs
   - Behaviors: how they currently approach the problem
   - Scenario: a supported account or explicitly hypothetical example, never a fabricated observed day
   - Design implications: what this means for product decisions
4. **Prioritize**: Recommend priorities only where the user’s objective and evidence justify them; do not silently make one segment more important or discard another agreed audience.
5. **Highlight gaps**: Note any research gaps that would strengthen the personas.
6. Present supported personas in the requested format or existing project record. A long answer does not require a new file.
