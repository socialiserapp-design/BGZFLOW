# Faux-UI restraint

An illustrative product surface needs enough context to explain the requested feature, without distracting detail. Preserve the approved style and the information the audience must understand. A real functional screen needs its actual content and behavior, not illustration shortcuts.

## The word budget

Choose readable strings by their role: title, action, input and result. The source uses 3–7 as a compact illustration example, not a limit. Preserve domain content such as album art, thumbnails and avatars when it carries meaning.

Placeholder bars can quiet peripheral illustrative content, but should not replace the subject, hide a missing feature or appear as real customer data. Match their line boxes and vary widths naturally (the source examples are 60, 45, 70, 52). Keep labels and values coherent. Repetition and multiple large numbers can be useful when the actual comparison requires them; use hierarchy rather than a fixed count. Use fictional names only where clearly illustrative, never as fabricated customers, contacts, testimonials or results.

## Fidelity is a dial

`minimal` can use bars, `standard` readable story content with quieter surroundings, and `realistic` fuller illustrative detail. Choose the degree needed by the brief and actual shown size; none is a default. Engineering can implement an agreed direction, but an unresolved consequential product or aesthetic choice belongs to the user. Do not silently turn an actual product UI into a simplified mock.

## Type and spacing

Check legibility at the actual rendered size, including responsive scaling. Source examples use 11–15px illustrative text, 12px insets, 8px field gaps, 4–6px title gaps and 6px around dots. These are recipe values, not accessibility guarantees or mandatory minimums. Use the product's type system and appropriate target sizes.

A purely decorative grouped illustration can use non-heading elements and an accessible summary without polluting the page outline. Meaningful content and interactive controls retain correct headings, labels, keyboard behavior and semantics; do not flatten a working UI into `role="img"`.

Inspect near-tangents, label centering and nested padding at shown size. Optical alignment (`text-box` where supported, or a hanging currency sign) and outer-radius = inner-radius + padding are useful options, not reasons to replace a coherent existing system.

## Icons, marks and colour

Prefer compatible existing product assets and a coherent icon language. Custom accessible SVG and purposeful emoji can also fit; no blanket authorship, icon-size or palette ban applies. Inspect translucent stroke overlaps and contrast on the actual ground.

Use third-party marks accurately and with appropriate rights, never to imply a false endorsement or integration. A real name need not acquire an invented logo. Sparkles, purple, shadows, translucent surfaces and accent rings are creative options when they serve the approved identity and remain readable. Status meanings must not rely on colour alone.

## Chrome and devices

Match the platform and fidelity the brief calls for. Use available authorized screenshots or current platform guidance rather than assuming remembered chrome is correct; do not open personal apps or add device work outside scope. Accurate system UI matters for a realistic study, while a clearly stylized illustration may simplify it. Account for actual safe areas and system overlays. iOS Messages conventions are not a default for every messaging product or phone.

## When to apply

Building or reviewing the still frame of any faux UI, and the poster frame of an animated one.

## Gotcha

Stripping the screen to two blobs is the opposite failure. If it looks emptier, flatter or smaller than the closest reference shot, enlarge the content and add structure before adding words ([screenshot-critique](../anti-patterns/screenshot-critique.md) has the same lesson for pages).
