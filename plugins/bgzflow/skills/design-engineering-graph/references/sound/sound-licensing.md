# Sound licensing

Identify the actual use: commercial or otherwise, private or published, edited or synchronized, and its delivery platform. Check the authoritative item page and exact licence/version, not an aggregator or a pack-wide assumption. This reference is source guidance, not a legal determination for every project.

## Licence checks

| Licence or label | Check for the actual intended use |
|---|---|
| CC0 | Confirm it applies to this recording; other rights may still matter. Retain provenance even where credit is not required. |
| Public Domain Mark | A status label, not the CC0 dedication. Check the work and applicable jurisdiction rather than treating the labels as interchangeable. |
| CC BY | Follow the applicable attribution, licence-link and change-indication requirements; check the exact version. |
| CC BY-NC | Confirm non-commercial use; a prototype for a commercial project is not automatically permitted. |
| CC BY-ND | Check adaptation and sharing restrictions. Creative Commons treats music synchronized to moving images as an adaptation; separate permission may be needed. |
| CC BY-SA | Adaptations require the same or a compatible licence. Assess whether the intended distribution can meet those terms; this is not a blanket ban on all ShareAlike assets. |
| Stock-library/model terms | Verify the actual item, account entitlement, commercial/editing/sync permissions, credits and redistribution restrictions. “Royalty free” alone is insufficient. |
| Conflicting labels | Resolve against authoritative rights-holder terms; do not invent permission from a convenient summary. Use a compatible alternative if unresolved. |

Creative Commons' [official FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/) and [music-for-video guidance](https://creativecommons.org/legalmusicforvideos/) explain these distinctions. For an uncertain consequential use, obtain the missing permission or appropriate advice instead of claiming certainty.

## Verify the actual item

- Freesound licences vary by recording. Check its item page, author, title, licence/version and relevant description; the author's other sounds can differ.
- Keep the authoritative source link, applicable terms and required credits in the project's existing asset records. Do not create a second mandatory receipt system.
- Check the final mix's actual contents, including later-added notes or effects, rather than relying on an earlier proposed track list.
- Downloading, publishing, account access and paid generation still follow existing task authority; reading this reference grants none of them.

## Credits and platform claims

Follow exact attribution requirements and choose a reasonable placement for the medium. A video description or linked credit page may fit, but do not assume on-screen credit is never required. Indicate changes where required.

Content ID and permission are different questions: permitted use can still receive an automated claim. Retain evidence and follow the actual platform process within existing authority; do not automatically dispute a claim or register third-party audio as exclusively owned. Check each stock provider's current restrictions before registration.

## Generated and procedural sound

Check current service/model output terms and account rights for provider-generated audio. For procedural audio distinguish code licences, sampled inputs and output rights; do not label every generated file CC0 automatically. A rights holder can choose an output licence only for rights they control.
