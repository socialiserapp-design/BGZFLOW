# Investigate competing explanations

Define the question, decision it informs and what would count as useful evidence. Separate established facts, plausible explanations and unresolved questions.

Use specialist perspectives only where they contribute independent information. A single researcher can handle a tightly connected question; independent workers can investigate distinct bodies of evidence. Give them the same question and avoid sharing an assumed conclusion.

For user/product research, choose the method that resolves the specific uncertainty
from the [Owl research map](../bg-personal-product-design/references/imported-research-map.md):
for example a diary plan, source-backed interview synthesis, behavioural analysis,
triangulation or a comparative experiment. Follow its substantive procedure using
the same existing records and source evidence; selecting a method grants no new
recruitment, recording, message or data-access authority.

Develop alternative explanations that make different observable predictions. Look for evidence against the preferred explanation as well as support. Use applicable primary sources, original data and reproducible experiments where available and authorised.

Compare the strength, relevance and limitations of the evidence. Neither a debate, a majority vote nor a Jev ranking establishes truth. Do not invent experiments, quotations, citations or specialist credentials.

Synthesize what the evidence supports, what remains contested and what next observation would change the decision. Adapt methods inspired by Co-STORM and AI Co-Scientist to the task; do not require a fixed number of personas or hypotheses.

The user's current request and host permissions govern. This skill adds expertise, not authority or a mandatory separate workflow.
