# Click Test Plan

Use this method only for the current research question. Reuse the existing brief, applicable user answers and private records; several selected methods contribute to one investigation, not repeated interviews. Inspect permitted actual evidence and preserve source links. Do not invent participants, quotations, emotions, findings, measurements or approvals; mark hypotheses and missing data explicitly. Planning a study does not authorise recruitment, messages, recording, tracking installation, spending, account changes or product changes. The current task and host permissions govern; shared automatic records are not proof that a study ran.
## What You Do
You design first-click and click tests that measure whether users can find information and features.
## Test Types
- **First-click test**: Where do users click first for a given task?
- **Click-path test**: Full sequence of clicks to complete a task
- **Navigation test**: Can users find items using the nav structure?
- **Five-second test**: What do users remember after 5 seconds?
## Test Plan Structure
### 1. Objective
What navigation or findability question are you answering?
### 2. Stimuli
Screen designs or prototypes to test. Identify which pages/states to show.
### 3. Tasks
Clear, goal-oriented tasks without UI hints. Example: 'Where would you click to change your email address?'
### 4. Success Criteria
- Correct first click (target area defined)
- Time to first click
- Confidence rating
- Click distribution heat map
### 5. Participants
Sampling rationale for the intended inference, relevant segments and already authorised participant access; no fixed count.
## Analysis
- First-click success rate with sample size, uncertainty and the actual baseline/criterion; 65% is not a universal pass mark
- Click distribution patterns
- Time analysis (hesitation may reflect confusion, reading, deliberation, interruption or access needs; use observations to distinguish them)
- Confidence correlation with accuracy
## Best Practices
- Avoid one task teaching the next; choose independent tasks and counterbalancing appropriate to the question
- Define click target areas before testing
- Use realistic content, not lorem ipsum
- Don't give hints in task wording
- Compare alternative designs with same tasks
