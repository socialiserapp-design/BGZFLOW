# Jev for scope, questions and risk

Choose locally first. For a justified Jev consultation, use the installed `judge --stdin --live` helper with a stable `taskId` from the original task brief. Reuse that identity across follow-ups, workers and compaction; do not generate a new identity to obtain another call. The helper allows one attempt and reuses an identical successful answer. On failure, uncertainty or changed input after use, continue locally and ask before another paid request. Do not use the multi-pass catalogue router or bypass the helper with a direct SDK call. Embedding applications must enforce the same budget in their trusted execution layer.


Use the [shared Jev method](../bg-jev/references/methods.md) and whichever existing
[risky changes](../risky-changes/SKILL.md) or [risky decisions](../risky-decisions/SKILL.md)
method fits. They own the risk procedure. For questioning, use the existing shared
interview method and relevant earlier answers, not another compulsory questionnaire.

Supply the latest request, relevant explicit decisions, proposed action, affected
parties and known effects. Ask concrete independent questions: does the proposed
change add a user-visible restriction; does it alter a saved decision; is a material
choice unresolved; is recovery known; what evidence supports the claimed effect?
Use Jev for interpretation, while exact paths, permissions, spending and destructive
operations stay under host/application controls. Check all actual execution routes
in an application; a hook on one tool does not secure another tool.

If a material preference is missing, the main agent explains the choice, practical
consequences, uncertainty and recommendation in plain English, then uses the user's
answer. Questions may exceed sixteen when needed; no count or score proves clarity.
Investigate facts first and don't re-ask settled questions. Simple requests do not
need a risk call just because this capability exists.

Never infer consent from confidence, classify a failed API call as safe, or create
a new customer-facing restriction without the user's authority. A suspected risk
needs evidence and a proportionate response. Preserve relevant observations through
the existing records; do not introduce receipts, a circuit breaker or stop-word gates.
