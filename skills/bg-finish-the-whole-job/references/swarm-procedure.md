# Swarm procedure

Read this and the [shared swarm rules](swarm-rules.md) when you dispatch parallel work or combine its results. Build whole, then test whole. Keep the work graph, receipts and integration queue in the project's existing records under `<project>/.bgzflow/`. Use the [glossary](../../bg-build-with-me/references/glossary.md) meanings.

Before dispatch, follow [shared rules 1–4](swarm-rules.md): discover installation/authentication/proof state, recommend only proven routes, ask once and save this swarm's approval. Qualify approved unproven routes before real work. Checkpoint measured concurrency, allowance/reset, ceiling and check/fix reserve, counting shared quotas once. Reuse approval and wave counts across nested commands; prior swarms supply recommendations only. Allocate ready work on approved proven suitable routes with effective model/effort/access verified. The shared dispatch checkpoint and cloud transport procedure apply to every wave; collect cloud commits untouched and fence/reconcile old writers before reassignment.

Two owners run a swarm. The **controller** (the lead) owns dispatch and pickup. The **integration writer** owns the combined candidate. Name both in the OWNERS block of the one-page [checkpoint](../../../templates/CHECKPOINT.md); one session may hold both roles when capacity requires it. Update the checkpoint at every state change. Move history to `ARCHIVE.md` and never delete it.

## From approved plan to one candidate

1. **Recover and allocate.** Reconcile current owners, live workers, job and request IDs, workspaces, dirty and untracked changes, accepted decisions and pending external effects. A `running` or `queued` record whose process is dead is uncertain, not active: apply the [dead-process check](worker-packet.md#dispatch-and-pickup). Pin the exact approved plan, requirements, designs and holds. Record each account's allowance and the once-agreed usage ceiling from supported readings or a dated report from the founder; ask only for missing information. Use the default [worker route](../../bg-build-with-me/references/worker-route.md): the lead plans, dispatches, reads short results and decides; workers build, test and integrate; fresh independent checkers check. Reserve the ceiling; label other verified routes as alternatives. Keep `<project>/.bgzflow/` under version control, or snapshot it before each wave.
2. **Wave 0: common ground.** One owner pins the existing glossary and C4 IDs, interface and data contracts, error, identity and compatibility behaviour, and acceptance fixtures. Put shared stubs and a minimal runnable customer path on the integration baseline. Name its single writer, record the branch, ref or artifact identity, and run the baseline checks. Keep baseline failures on record and scope their repairs. Lock the approved look; request only a material change to it.
3. **Prepare isolated lanes.** Reuse suitable owned workspaces or create each worker branch from the recorded wave-0 SHA with an explicit start-point, after preparing shared dependencies. Verify resolved path, branch, baseline, dirty state and owner. Preserve admitted prior commits for repairs. Every writer owns its worktree/output; read-only research may share a checkout. Isolate suite, journey and checkers separately at the complete candidate SHA with separate data/resources under shared rule 10. An isolation failure blocks the affected lane. Worktrees do not isolate credentials, devices or services.
4. **Admit and dispatch the ready set.** Validate the [graph invariants](work-graph.md#admission-and-integration-rules) and fill one [worker packet](worker-packet.md) per task. When a dependent needs a prerequisite's code, the controller first checks the prerequisite's fast-check evidence, then stacks the dependent on its full pinned commit and records a consumer-specific `accepted-dependency` and the dependent's baseline. Admitting a build never needs, or marks, final integration. Select and load the required skills, carry the lead's permissions subject to host limits, and verify effective settings where the host shows them. After plan approval, launch every ready independent lane that fits the host's slots, usable provider capacity, resource ownership, integration throughput and the usage ceiling. Launch local command-line workers with `bg-swarm launch` (the prompt is always a file; a launch counts only once the process is alive and writing output), see them with `bg-swarm status`, and end one with `bg-swarm stop`. Do mechanical steps under about 2 minutes yourself instead of dispatching them. Record returned worker and request IDs and actual running intervals. Never create extra sessions to get around a limit.
5. **Keep the lead informed through the route.** Record each task's real identity, owner, workspace, activity, state, latest meaningful result, findings and next action. On the default route the lead sees short status and results while full worker histories stay saved; separate worker chats or a live board are optional. Other routes verify their own board or transcript controls where their visibility rule requires them. Arm one background wait on native completion, then read the exact result, both with the original launch cwd. Use the route's commands, not a home-made wrapper. Use no polling loop, no forwarding agent and no routine log or transcript reads. Record a failure or timeout and reconcile the same job; a timeout is not a cancellation. Refresh usage at material checkpoints and near the ceiling, never per message. Once per lead session, validate the actual job directory and completion-watcher path before relying on them; record the resolved paths and the result, and hold the affected dispatch or wait if either is invalid. Start each new stage in a fresh short chat from the checkpoint and a handoff of `BGZFLOW_HANDOFF_KB` (2 KB) or less.

**Build first, check once.** Steps 6 to 10 never review single jobs and never run the full suite per merge. Reviewing every job and running the full suite after each merge turns a parallel swarm into a single-file queue of reviews and repair rounds.

Wave 0 pins the whole-journey acceptance script and expected results. Every build/fix worker reads and uses test-driven-development, systematic-debugging and verification-before-completion, with actual read receipts or the explicit equivalent-method gap in [shared rule 7](swarm-rules.md). Its handback includes proof/confidence. Merge all exact contributions, including untouched cloud commits, and pin the complete SHA before the suite/journey in isolated checkouts. Collect both runtime results before independent review. Every fix wave merges all repair branches and pins a new SHA before one suite/journey run and the affected independent re-check. Never retest the old SHA. Reuse only unchanged exact-candidate evidence; `/swarm-check` is no reason to repeat it.

6. **Workers build and run only fast checks.** Workers read their packet and selected skill bodies, implement only owned changes, and run only fast checks on their own files: type check, lint and the unit tests they touched. They never run the full suite and never use a shared test database. They fix their fast-check failures in the same job and commit on their own branch. They return the exact commit or artifact (or a preserved patch or worktree, including untracked files), command outputs and environment. They report blockers early and keep independent work going. A worker never waives a required test and never marks its own candidate accepted.
7. **Collect built branches.** Pick up finished, failed and interrupted returns promptly. The integration writer records each branch, its fast-check output and changed paths in the integration queue. A dependent that needs another package's code builds in a stacked checkout at that prerequisite's admitted full commit SHA; resolve a named branch once and verify the pin at launch. Contract-only work may build on wave 0. Nothing is marked `integrated` yet, and no reviewer sees single jobs. Start newly ready tasks as slots free.
8. **One integration job, one full run.** When every package is built, one integration job verifies the destination head and merges all branches in dependency order, by exact commit, through the project's merge queue or a local integration branch. It resolves conflicts by keeping both behaviours and asks the original owners only when the two behaviours truly conflict. It never overwrites unrelated dirty work. It checks that every accepted `DECISIONS.md` ID is present in the combined head, then runs the full test suite and critical customer journeys once, through `bg-heavy -- <command>` so the machine runs one heavy job at a time. If the run fails, it finds the merge that broke it by rerunning the failing tests on the combined head without that branch, and records the finding against that branch's owner. Mark a contribution `integrated` only after its exact commits are on the combined head and the required combined checks pass. Failed combined checks keep the affected contributions `repair-required` with their findings; recording a finding is not integration success. Before handback, record in the project ledger every clone or worktree and every integrated commit SHA, with its resolved path, owner, branch and integration destination.
9. **Check once, then re-integrate every fix wave.** After collecting suite/journey results, run one independent check on the complete pinned SHA through [BG Check It Before Release](../../bg-check-it-before-release/SKILL.md). Use up to 3 fresh read-only checkers by area (money/logins/consent/data; UI; everything else), each in its own pinned checkout with isolated resources, never a builder's cwd/resume pool. Prefer a different proven model where available. Verify accepted `DECISIONS.md` IDs and original required outputs. Keep stable finding IDs, requirements, reproduction and owner. Verify repair identities, then send one parallel same-owner batch. Every wave merges all fix branches, pins a new SHA, reruns real-platform golden journeys and rollback on it, never another review; never retest the old SHA. Preserve `bg-rounds` counts across nested commands/resumes: at most 2 waves. Report remaining IDs with recommendations; wave 3 needs explicit founder approval. Safe independent work continues, but failed checks or blocking findings prevent customer release. Reconcile unknown effects and fence old writers before retry/reassignment.
10. **Skeleton, then the whole product.** Show the founder one early working end-to-end skeleton and name its mocked or pending parts. Record material decisions in `DECISIONS.md` and keep authorised work going; the skeleton is not a gate for unrelated lanes. Brand-new projects get this one early skeleton check; everything else waits for the combined result. The lead reads the short verdicts and runs the cheapest decisive proof. On a `ready` verdict the lead accepts the merged result itself, records it and continues; it never asks the founder to accept a review. It asks the founder only about undecided product, design or business choices, spending, release outside the brief's authority, or findings still open after 2 fix waves, each with a recommendation. Worker completion, lead acceptance, release authority and production verification stay separate. Release needs existing authority and [BG Ship and Recover](../../bg-ship-and-recover/SKILL.md).

## Route controls

Discover the host's actual tool schema before calling it. The names here describe supported routes, not a portable API. Record a missing control as a named gap; never install a daemon, timer or orchestration framework for it.

### Default route: lead to worker jobs

Follow the [worker route](../../bg-build-with-me/references/worker-route.md) and the host's capability card in [ROUTES.md](../../../templates/ROUTES.md) for the readiness check (once per lead chat), direct dispatch, one background wait, exact result pickup and the resume-candidate preview. Apply the founder's route overrides from the overlay's `ROUTES.md`. Keep the card's default model unless the brief authorises another, and keep the requested effort. Verify actual permissions and network access; a write flag proves no inheritance. Report a mismatch and hold only the affected operation; leave host policy unchanged.

**Identity.** Bind identity before launch, after launch and before resume exactly as the [route receipt rules](work-graph.md#route-receipt-extension) set out: name the return route before launch, keep unlaunched IDs null with the reason `not-yet-launched`, and never redispatch only to obtain an ID. Before resuming a worker thread, require the exact job, thread, lead session, cwd, workspace and base from that launch, and verify effective write access and the Git common directory against the recorded assignment; a null, unknown or mismatched value blocks only that resume. Save evidence outside any rolling job index before it is pruned; retention is not a concurrency cap.

**Repair.** Preview the exact resume candidate with the route's supported command and compare every recorded identity field, including the intended thread. Older jobs launched from a shared parent folder keep that folder as their identity, even when later briefs launch in worktrees. A "resume last" control picks the latest compatible terminal job, not the named worker: never use it blind from a shared parent folder. Changing cwd never moves old history. Resume only on an exact match with no conflicting active assignment. Otherwise preserve the candidate, dirty files and finding lineage, hold only that repair, and ask for supported exact continuation. Start a successor repair job only after an explicit terminal-job handoff fences the old owner and reconciles pending effects; record its identity honestly. Never take over a writer another app or chat owns, edit route state, or replay blindly.

**Pickup.** The lead owns pickup of completed, failed and interrupted results. One background wait receives native completion; collect the exact result and keep failure or timeout output, remaining running state, next owner and action. If native idle notification is unqualified, record that gap and checkpoint; never build a polling service. Repairs already authorised need no repeat question to the founder. Messaging between independent peer leads is a separate, separately qualified route; ordinary worker dispatch needs no peer bridge. Shared capability and record limits live in [enforcement](../../bg-check-it-before-release/references/enforcement.md); keep working recovery until its replacement passes. Earlier reviews stay source evidence; they never become a new mandatory gate or a second controller.

### Host-native subagents (alternatives)

Use a host's own subagent tools only after reading its current version and schema. The same rules apply.

- **Dispatch** a bounded packet and record the returned agent ID. A fork without history sees only the packet, so supply complete context. A full-history fork inherits the parent's model and effort; override them only when authorised and supported.
- **Collect** the completion notification and the final answer, and correlate them with the exact worker and candidate before advancing the graph. Steer a running worker only through its message control; repair an idle one through the same-agent follow-up control. After an interrupt, reconcile outstanding tools and effects first.
- **Isolate** explicitly; native subagents may share the parent's folder. Use managed worktree tools attached to the intended repository. Creating a worktree does not move the chat, so pass the returned workspace to the worker. The default base may be the remote default branch: name the approved integration ref and confirm the worktree starts there, not at the parent's HEAD. Keep owned work until no active task needs it, then archive it with managed controls.
- **Fill real capacity.** Read the host's concurrency maximum; it may include the controller, so four total slots allow at most three other live agents, fewer if one is busy. Dispatch at that maximum and refill as lanes finish. It is a host limit, not a policy cap; shared subscription limits still apply. Create named, user-owned chats only when the user asks.
- **Verify, never assume.** Check permission inheritance and effective settings against the lead and host policy. Tools prove dispatch, status and return only. When the route needs a board or full transcript, inspect the host's actual view and confirm finished conversations stay readable through the handoff; a tool list or saved board file proves no live visibility. A native wait inside an active turn does not prove the lead resumes after a restart or wakes while idle. Named subagents may behave differently with team features on, and experimental teams may not restore live teammates after a lead restart; verify actual isolation. Documented controls are not proof they ran in this session.

Host notes, e.g.: a Claude Code-style `Agent` tool (background runs, task list, `isolation: worktree`); a Codex-style host (spawn, list, wait, message and interrupt agent tools, managed worktrees). Exact tool names and calling rules go on the host's route card.

### PC-off cloud work and gaps

Follow the [account and cloud checklist](../../bg-continue-my-project/references/account-cloud-checklist.md): included entitlement with additional spend at zero unless authorised; private repository, environment, tools and secret scope; one writer; exact return proof. A local background job is local, not PC-off cloud work. Work that needs local emulators, local-only files, signing or devices is `waiting-for-PC`; never claim local processes continue after shutdown. Pre-authorise push for cloud workers in the brief, or have them commit to their branch every N minutes. On return, reconcile cloud results, local processes, workspace ownership and uncertain requests before resuming.

Record each unsupported need as a named gap with its affected tasks and a safe route: history or board access, idle return or wake, a per-job hard quota, a requested model, repository isolation, a cloud environment, or same-session account resume. Keep an active supported wait or a compact continuation checkpoint instead of a polling loop. Messages between peer leads on different hosts need the project's verified exact-recipient queue or urgent-steer route; native child-agent tools do not establish it. Keep the existing peer-wake procedure for routes that need it until a qualified replacement exists.

## Worked example

Illustrative, not a live run. The approved outcome is a notes app: `REQ-NOTE-01` saves a note, `REQ-NOTE-02` lists saved notes, and `REQ-NOTE-03` handles a storage failure without losing entered text. The project's existing IDs and contracts stay canonical.

| Task | Prerequisites | Owned changes | Tests and handback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T0, integration writer | Approved plan | `contracts/notes.json`, shared test fixture, runnable shell | Pin `CONTRACT-NOTES-v1`; run baseline; integrate at H0. |
| T1, storage worker | T0 integrated | `src/storage/`, storage tests | Fast checks only (type check, lint, storage unit tests); commit C1 on its branch. |
| T2, UI worker | T0 integrated | `src/screens/notes/`, UI tests | Fast checks only (type check, lint, UI unit tests with the contract mock); commit C2 on its branch. |
| T3, integration job | T1 and T2 built | Integration harness, customer-flow evidence | Merge C1 and C2 at H1; run the full suite and critical journeys once. |

H0, H1, H2, C1, C2, C2a, J1, J2 and the thread labels are placeholders, not receipts.

1. T0 pins `CONTRACT-NOTES-v1`, passes the baseline checks and is integrated at H0. Only then do T1 and T2 become ready. They own disjoint paths and build in parallel; a live run records overlapping native activity intervals rather than inferring overlap from two dispatch calls. H0 is the one early skeleton, with its mocked parts labelled.
2. T1 (job J1, thread-1) and T2 (job J2, thread-2) each return a committed branch with passing fast checks. Nobody reviews either job on its own.
3. With both built, T3 merges C1 and C2 at H1, confirms the accepted decisions are present, and runs the full suite and critical journeys once. The storage-failure journey fails: entered text is lost. Rerunning it on H0 + C1 alone passes, so the merge of C2 broke it. T3 records F-NOTE-01 against T2 and logs both worktrees and H1 in the project ledger.
4. The independent check runs once on H1: a storage-and-data checker and a UI checker in parallel, both fresh, read-only and a different model from the builders. The UI checker also finds F-NOTE-02: the error message is not announced to screen readers. The verdict is `changes-required`.
5. Fix wave 1: F-NOTE-01 and F-NOTE-02 go in one batch to T2's verified thread-2, which returns C2a. T3 merges it at H2 and reruns the real-platform golden journeys and rollback. Both findings close with H2 rehearsal outputs, never another review.
6. The H2 rehearsal passes with no open reproduced blocker. The lead accepts H2 and records it without asking the founder to accept the review. After fix wave 2, open IDs go to the founder with a recommendation; safe independent work continues. Failed checks or blocking findings prevent customer release, which still needs its own authority and artifact/production identity proof.
