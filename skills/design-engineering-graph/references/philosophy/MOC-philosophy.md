# MOC — Philosophy

The cluster that answers "why bother." Use when the user asks why something feels off, when picking between two competing approaches, or when justifying a polish investment.

## Nodes

- [taste-is-trained](taste-is-trained.md) — Taste is a trained instinct, not innate. Reference libraries, constraints, and time spent looking are the inputs.
- [unseen-details-compound](unseen-details-compound.md) — The details users never consciously notice are the ones that decide whether they trust the product.
- [beauty-is-leverage](beauty-is-leverage.md) — Polish compounds into competitive moat. The thing that looks like it doesn't matter is often the thing that does.
- [delight-impact-curve](delight-impact-curve.md) — Potential for delight rises as feature frequency falls. Daily-use features need minimal delight; rare moments earn big ones.
- [feeling-right](feeling-right.md) — Optimize for "feeling right," not just "working." This is the part the model cannot infer from training data.
- [marketing-vs-product-ui](marketing-vs-product-ui.md) — Marketing is brand expression and narrative pacing. Product is consistency for the 100th session, not the first. Different rules apply.
- [states-are-the-work](states-are-the-work.md) — Loading, empty, error, partial, offline, permission-denied. The real design surface is the states, not the happy path.
- [data-is-content](data-is-content.md) — Tables, charts, lists, dashboards deserve the same typography, alignment, and whitespace care as marketing pages.
- [dependency-discipline](dependency-discipline.md) — Compare existing packages, platform APIs and custom code by capability, correctness, maintenance and measured cost; do not reimplement merely because a snippet is short.
- [pointing-beats-describing](pointing-beats-describing.md) — Precise machine-readable selection (selectors, file paths, coordinates) outperforms natural-language description when feedback goes to an AI agent.
- [articulate-precisely](articulate-precisely.md) — Name the phenomenon exactly, and say why it works. Precise vocabulary is what converts taste into a spec — for a teammate or for an agent.
