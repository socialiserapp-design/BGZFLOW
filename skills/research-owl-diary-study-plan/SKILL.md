# Diary Study Plan

Use this method only for the current research question. Reuse the existing brief, applicable user answers and private records; several selected methods contribute to one investigation, not repeated interviews. Inspect permitted actual evidence and preserve source links. Do not invent participants, quotations, emotions, findings, measurements or approvals; mark hypotheses and missing data explicitly. Planning a study does not authorise recruitment, messages, recording, tracking installation, spending, account changes or product changes. The current task and host permissions govern; shared automatic records are not proof that a study ran.

Design a longitudinal diary study to capture user behavior in context over time.

## Instructions

1. **Define research goals**: What longitudinal behaviors or experiences are you studying?
2. **Design the study**:
   - **Duration**: Match the behaviour cycle and participant burden; one to four weeks is illustrative, not required
   - **Participants**: Sampling rationale and screening for the decision, anticipated burden and any existing compensation authority; no fixed count or permission to recruit/pay
   - **Entry prompts**: Daily or event-triggered prompts (mix of structured and open-ended)
   - **Capture methods**: Choose minimum necessary capture with actual consent, access and retention boundaries; a plan does not authorise recording
   - **Check-in schedule**: Mid-study interviews or pulse surveys
   - **Onboarding**: Participant briefing, practice entries, tool setup
3. **Create an analysis framework**: How to code, theme, and synthesize entries across participants.
4. **Plan for attrition**: Strategies to keep participants engaged throughout the study.
5. Distinguish authorised preparation from participant access, scheduling or collection still unresolved.
